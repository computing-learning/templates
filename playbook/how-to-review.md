# How to Review Knowledge

## Goal

Retain knowledge by recalling it from memory at increasing intervals rather than repeatedly rereading notes.

---

# Default Review Schedule

```text id="u7jgsp"
Day 1
↓
Day 3
↓
Day 7
↓
Day 14
↓
Day 30
↓
Day 90
```

This schedule is a guideline rather than a strict rule.

* Difficult topics may require shorter intervals.
* Easy topics may be reviewed less frequently.

---

# Review Rules

Do **not** begin by rereading your notes.

First, try to recall the topic entirely from memory.

Use questions such as:

* What problem does this solve?
* How does it work?
* What are its main components?
* What are the trade-offs?
* When should it be used?
* When should it be avoided?
* What can fail?
* What topics are related?

Only after answering should you compare your explanation with your original notes.

---

# Review Result

Rate each review using one of four outcomes:

```text id="mpsuzw"
Again
Hard
Good
Easy
```

Suggested behavior:

| Result | Next Review                       |
| ------ | --------------------------------- |
| Again  | Review tomorrow                   |
| Hard   | Review in 2–3 days                |
| Good   | Continue the normal schedule      |
| Easy   | Increase the next review interval |

The goal is to spend more time reviewing topics that are difficult and less time on concepts that are already well understood.

---

# Review Output

Record only useful observations.

Include:

* What was remembered.
* What was forgotten.
* Incorrect assumptions.
* New questions.
* Next review date.

Avoid rewriting the original note.

The review record should capture learning progress rather than duplicate existing documentation.

# Review Workflow

```text id="rctwsm"
Recall from memory
        ↓
Answer review questions
        ↓
Compare with notes
        ↓
Identify gaps
        ↓
Rate the result
        ↓
Schedule the next review
```

---

# Review Principles

* Recall before rereading.
* Focus on understanding rather than memorization.
* Record only what changed.
* Turn forgotten concepts into new learning tasks.
* Let review intervals grow as confidence increases.

The objective of review is to strengthen long-term memory through repeated retrieval, not to repeatedly consume the same information.

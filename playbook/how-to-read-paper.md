# How to Read a Technical Paper

## Goal

Understand a technical paper efficiently by focusing on the problem, proposed solution, evidence, and practical relevance before studying implementation details.

---

# First Pass — Understand the Big Picture

Read only the high-level sections:

* Title
* Abstract
* Introduction
* Section headings
* Diagrams
* Conclusion

Do **not** study formulas, proofs, or implementation details yet.

Answer the following questions:

* What problem is being solved?
* Why is the problem important?
* What is the proposed idea?
* What are the claimed results?

The objective of the first pass is to determine whether the paper is relevant and understand its overall direction.

---

# Second Pass — Understand the Design

Read the technical sections:

* System model
* Assumptions
* Algorithm
* Evaluation
* Limitations

While reading:

* Record unfamiliar keywords.
* Highlight important concepts.
* Do not interrupt the reading flow to research every unknown term.

Unknown terms become future learning backlog items.

---

# Third Pass — Study the Details

Study the parts that require deeper understanding:

* Proofs
* Algorithms
* Experiments
* Edge cases
* Cited foundational work

Focus on understanding **why** the proposed approach works, not just **how** it is implemented.

---

# Paper Notes

Summarize the paper using the following structure:

```text id="v3ckm7"
Problem

Assumptions

Main Contribution

Architecture or Algorithm

Evidence

Limitations

Practical Relevance

Related Topics
```

Keep notes concise and written in your own words.

---

# Reading Workflow

```text id="d2s0pi"
First Pass
(Big Picture)
        ↓
Second Pass
(Core Design)
        ↓
Third Pass
(Deep Understanding)
        ↓
Write Paper Notes
        ↓
Extract Keywords
        ↓
Build Labs
        ↓
Apply the Ideas
```

---

# Reading Principles

* Read for understanding before reading for detail.
* Identify assumptions before evaluating conclusions.
* Distinguish claims from evidence.
* Record unanswered questions.
* Build experiments to verify important ideas.

A paper is not fully understood until you can explain its main contribution, discuss its limitations, and apply its ideas in practice.
